Here are three bullet points summarizing the document:

• Global warming refers to the phenomenon of increasing average air temperatures near the surface of Earth over the past one to two centuries, with human activities since at least the beginning of the Industrial Revolution having a growing influence on the pace and extent of present-day climate change.

• Climate scientists have gathered detailed observations of various weather phenomena and related influences on climate, indicating that Earth's climate has changed over almost every conceivable timescale since the beginning of geologic time. The best estimate of the increase in global average surface temperature between 1850 and 2019 is 1.07 °C (1.9 °F), with most of the warming over the second half of the 20th century attributed to human activities.

• The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) has produced reports highlighting the risks associated with global warming, including increased extinction of plant and animal species, shifts in patterns of agriculture, and rising sea levels. The IPCC's Sixth Assessment Report predicts that the global mean surface temperature will increase by between 1.0 and 3.5 °C (1.8 and 6.3 °F) by 2100 relative to the 1850-1900 average, depending on greenhouse gas emissions scenarios.
